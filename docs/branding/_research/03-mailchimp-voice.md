# Mailchimp Content Style Guide — voice & writing principles

Sources:
- `styleguide.mailchimp.com/voice-and-tone/` (WebFetch, 2026-06-04)
- `styleguide.mailchimp.com/writing-principles/` (WebFetch, 2026-06-04)

## Why Mailchimp is the voice-doc gold standard

Mailchimp's content style guide is the most-cited public voice doc in B2B
software. It's the framework Atlassian, GitLab, Shopify, etc. mostly model
themselves after. GAAhex needs its own voice doc, and Mailchimp's structure is
the right scaffold — even though our register is different (operator-grade,
not friendly-fluffy).

## Voice vs. Tone — the load-bearing distinction

**Voice** is constant. **Tone** changes based on context and the reader's
emotional state. Mailchimp's exact line: *"You have the same voice all the
time, but your tone changes."*

Lesson for GAAhex: this distinction is non-negotiable in the voice doc. Our
voice is one thing (operator-grade, honest, present). Our tone shifts:
- a successful provisioning toast → quiet confirmation
- a critical alarm → terse, precise, no softening
- an empty-state in Studio → instructional, mildly patient

## Mailchimp's four voice principles

1. **Plainspoken** — eliminate hyperbole; clarity first.
2. **Genuine** — relate authentically; warm but not familiar.
3. **Translators** — demystify complexity; educate.
4. **Dry humor** — *"weird but not inappropriate, smart but not snobbish."*

Lesson for GAAhex: principles 1, 2, 3 transfer directly. **Principle 4 does
not.** GAAhex is an ISP operator's console where a single wrong action can
take down service for thousands. Humor in chrome copy is a liability. We
replace "dry humor" with **"steady" — calm under load, no panic, no jokes
during alarms.**

## Mailchimp's tone principles

- Generally informal; clarity supersedes entertainment.
- Read the user's emotional state before choosing tone.
- Humor should feel natural; *"don't go out of your way to make a joke."*
- When uncertain, stay straightforward.

Lesson for GAAhex: drop the humor rules entirely. Keep "read the reader's
state" and "when uncertain, stay straightforward." Add: **assume the operator
is mid-incident** — that's the default emotional context for an ISP NOC tool.

## Mailchimp's operational style tips

- Use active voice exclusively.
- Avoid slang and jargon; plain English.
- Positive language over negative.

Lesson for GAAhex: active voice ✓. Avoid slang ✓. But "positive language"
needs nuance — if a service is down, the value text saying "Online" in red is
wrong. State accurately, don't sugarcoat. Add: **state the bad news directly;
don't soften severity.**

## Mailchimp's five content goals

Empower, Respect, Educate, Guide, Speak Truth.

Lesson for GAAhex: adopt all five as-is. They're durable. "Speak Truth" maps
to our honesty floor (`.md`). "Respect" maps to the
operator-grade audience — they are experts, treat them that way.

## Mailchimp's four content characteristics

1. **Clear** — *"Use simple words and sentences."*
2. **Useful** — what's the purpose; who reads this; what do they need.
3. **Friendly** — *"Write like a human."*
4. **Appropriate** — adapt tone to the situation.

Lesson for GAAhex: keep all four. Soften "Friendly" to **"Human"** — friendly
is a fluffier register than GAAhex's; "human" preserves the warmth without
the sweetness.
